Statement

Practice and methodology

A figure crouches. It is always the same figure and it is never the same figure. It attends to something small and precise inside a structure that has no walls.

Behind it, the shadow.

The house form is the oldest argument. The red circle arrived uninvited, as things do, and stayed. It doesn't explain itself. Neither does the figure. Neither does the work.

Someone is building something. Someone has always been building something. The materials are temporary. The intention is not nothing.

Ignorance is blunt. The altar stands anyway.

David Byrne is an Irish sculptor and digital artist based in Yukuhashi, Fukuoka Prefecture, Japan. Trained in Fine Art Sculpture at NCAD Dublin under Nigel Rolfe and Brian King, he has been making and exhibiting work since 1984. His current practice centres on two bodies of digital work — the Superposition series and Altar of Entropy — developed in Blender 3D and conceived for physical fabrication in steel, bronze, CNC-routed oak, and 3D-printed elements. All work is hand-modelled original sculpture. No AI generation tools are used at any stage of his process.
